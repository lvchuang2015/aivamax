JarveePro Daily Q&A Diary – April 14, 2026 | We Don’t Sell Accounts—We Provide Infrastructure

2026-04-14

Introduction

Not every customer conversation is meant to convert—and that’s a good thing.

In today’s JarveePro Daily Q&A, what looks like a simple pricing inquiry quickly turns into a deeper lesson about

positioning, expectations, and how serious providers separate themselves from commodity sellers

This isn’t just about Instagram accounts. It’s about understanding the difference between

buying assets

and

accessing a system designed to scale them

Q 1: Do you sell small Instagram accounts?

Answer:

We provide Instagram accounts, but not as a standalone product.

Accounts are supplied exclusively to clients who are actively using our automation and management systems. They’re part of a broader infrastructure designed for scaling—not individual items listed for public sale.

If you're looking to simply purchase accounts without a system behind them, we’re likely not the right fit.

Q 2: How much per account?

Our current allocation pricing is $50 per 100 accounts.

That said, pricing isn’t the core value here. The accounts are meant to be used within a structured environment that includes:

Account warm-up processes

Behavioral automation

Multi-account management

Without that framework, account performance and longevity are significantly reduced.

Q 3: Are they aged accounts?

No, these are newly created accounts.

However, they are intended to go through a proper warm-up and activity cycle within our system. In practice, how accounts are managed after creation has far more impact than whether they are aged at the start.

Q 4: Can you share usernames to verify?

Answer:

We don’t provide public samples or share usernames.

Accounts are distributed only within our client ecosystem and are part of a managed setup. We don’t operate as a marketplace where inventory is browsed or pre-verified externally.

If your workflow requires pre-checking usernames or selecting accounts individually, our structure won’t align with that approach.

Q 5: How can buyers trust the accounts without verification?

That’s a fair question—but it comes down to how the service is structured.

We’re not positioned as an open marketplace where trust is built through samples. Instead, accounts are one component of a larger system that clients use and validate through ongoing operation.

Clients who work within this model evaluate performance based on:

Stability over time

Integration with automation

Campaign results

If the expectation is to verify assets before entering the system, then the model itself may not be the right match.

Q 6: Is this how business should be done?

Answer:

It depends on the type of business.

Marketplace models prioritize:

Visibility

Selection

Pre-purchase validation

Infrastructure-based models (like ours) prioritize:

Controlled environments

System performance

Long-term scalability

We operate in the second category. The goal isn’t to sell accounts individually—it’s to support clients running multi-account operations efficiently.

Summary

This conversation highlights a common mismatch in the social media space:

Buyers often look for products

Providers like us deliver systems

Accounts, on their own, are easy to find.

Reliable infrastructure that keeps them running—that’s where the real value is.

We’re not trying to serve everyone.

We’re built for users who understand that

scaling isn’t about buying accounts—it’s about managing them correctly from day one

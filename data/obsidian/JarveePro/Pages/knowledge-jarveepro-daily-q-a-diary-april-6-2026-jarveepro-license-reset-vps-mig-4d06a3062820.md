Introduction

Switching to a new VPS is a common step when scaling or fixing instability in your automation setup. But it often comes with confusion—especially when your license suddenly becomes invalid or you’re unsure how to move all your existing campaigns and settings.

In this JarveePro Daily Q&A Diary, we break down a real user case and walk through exactly what happens when you change servers, how to fix license issues, and how to migrate your entire setup without losing progress.

Q1: Can I Use the Same JarveePro License After Moving to a New VPS?

Answer:

Yes—but not directly.

JarveePro licenses are tied to a single device or VPS. When you install JarveePro on a new server, the system detects it as a different machine, which is why your license shows as “invalid.”

To fix this, you need to

reset your license

Once the reset is done:

The old VPS is disconnected

The license becomes available again

You can activate it on your new VPS

There’s no need to buy a new license—just a reset is required.

Q2: How Can I Transfer All My Settings, Campaigns, Proxies, and Fingerprints to the New VPS?

You can migrate almost everything, but it requires manually copying your JarveePro data.

To do this:

Go to your old VPS and locate the JarveePro installation folder

Copy the full data directory (including accounts, campaigns, proxies, and settings)

Move it to your new VPS

Paste it into the same directory where JarveePro is installed

Make sure JarveePro is closed during the process

Launch JarveePro on the new VPS

This will restore your setup as it was.

Summary

Moving to a new VPS doesn’t mean starting from scratch.

If your license becomes invalid, it simply needs a reset to work on the new machine. And instead of rebuilding your campaigns manually, you can transfer your entire setup by copying the correct files.

With the right steps, you can:

Migrate campaigns and accounts

Continue running automation with minimal downtime

A better VPS combined with a properly migrated setup can significantly improve stability, performance, and long-term scalability.
